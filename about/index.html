Why I design

Products people depend on

I grew up where the simplest conveniences were often out of reach. That’s where my interest in design started: not in how things look, but in whether they work for the person who needs them.

I studied engineering and found my way to product design because I kept asking why things were built the way they were, and how they could work better. I’m drawn to products where getting it right isn’t aesthetic. It decides whether someone can see a doctor, manage their savings, or do their job without fighting the tool.

How I think about design

Three principles, and where they showed up

01

Outcomes over output

I judge work by what changed: support tickets, completion rates, time on task. Not by how many screens shipped.

↓30%Support tickets after rebuilding GoCo Reports→

02

Structure before subtraction

Expert users need depth. The fix is almost always better structure and progressive disclosure, not removing capability.

3×Faster time to insight at Lenox Park, with no data removed→

03

Trust is designed

In health and money, consistency isn’t decoration. It’s how people decide whether a product is safe to rely on.
