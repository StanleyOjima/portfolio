Part one

Three years across the platform

Breadth across the product, the system underneath it, and how the team made decisions.

Overview

HR software where a mistake lands in someone’s paycheck

GoCo serves small and mid-sized U.S. businesses that rarely have an HR department. They have one overloaded person who onboards hires, approves leave, runs payroll and enrolls people in benefits, often in the same afternoon. Every ambiguous screen turns into a support ticket, a payroll correction or a benefits error.

I joined in July 2022 as one of two product designers, partnering with eight product managers across several engineering teams. When a Director of Design joined two years in, I worked with them to change how the team makes decisions. I stayed through the Intuit acquisition in early 2025.

Part two

Deep dive: rebuilding Reports

The feature customers trusted least, why it failed, and the three calls that turned it around.

The problem

Reports had stopped being trusted

HR managers use reports to answer questions they’re accountable for: headcount, pay, leave, benefits. GoCo’s builder showed every option on one screen with no preview, so the only way to check a report was to export it and look. When people can’t trust a report, they stop using it and start filing tickets.

Step 1

Open the builder

Every field, filter and option competes on one screen.

<28%monthly usage, the lowest of any major feature
Step 2

Configure without feedback

No preview, and filter logic most admins couldn’t explain.

Step 3

Export to verify

The only way to see a result was to download it.

2–3exports before a report was right
Step 4

Correct and repeat

Back into the builder, guessing which setting was wrong.

Step 5

Escalate to support

When the numbers couldn’t be trusted, a ticket was the fallback.

30%of all support tickets

My role

Owned end to end, from research to handoff

I led Reports from the first interview to the handoff spec, with the product manager and the engineers who owned reporting.

What I owned

Research through spec

Interviews, ticket analysis, the competitive audit, the flow, prototypes, usability tests and the spec.

Who I worked with

PM and reporting engineers

Scope and sequencing with product; query feasibility and performance with engineering.

Constraint

Nothing could break

Thousands of saved reports had to open in the new builder without migration.

Constraint

One release to earn trust back

The first release had to kill the export loop, not just look better.

What we learned

The data was fine. The sequence wasn’t.

We spoke to six HR managers, analysed support tickets, and audited how other reporting tools handle complexity.

6 interviews

Trial and error, every time

Every manager described the same loop: guess the filters, export, check, start again.

Support data

Two failure types, 30% of volume

Both were builder problems, not data problems.

Competitive audit

Everyone else reveals options in steps

Looker, Mode and even Google Sheets use progressive disclosure. GoCo showed everything at once.

The insight

The builder asked people to make every decision at once and gave them no way to see the result. The fix wasn’t fewer options. It was the right order, and immediate feedback.

Key decisions & trade-offs

Three calls that shaped the new Reports

Decision 01

Sequence the decisions instead of removing them

The call

Fields, then filters, then schedule, each on its own step, with advanced options collapsed until someone asks. Power users keep every capability; new users meet them one at a time.

The trade-off

A wizard was easier to build but locked experienced users into a path they didn’t need. A sidebar kept everything on one screen and left no room for a preview. AI-suggested reports were tempting and too risky for a trust problem. Tabbed steps guide without taking control away.

Decision 02

Pay for the preview in engineering, not in support

The call

Results update live as people configure, so a wrong filter shows up in seconds instead of after an export.

The trade-off

A “dry run” button would have been cheaper, and it would have kept the loop generating the tickets: click, wait, check, go back. I made the case that real-time queries would cost less than the support load, and engineering took on the harder build.

Decision 03

Defaults over templates

The call

Every report type opens with its most common fields already selected. Time to a first report dropped by more than half.

The trade-off

Templates looked helpful but added a decision before any value, and boxed people in once chosen. Defaults are a starting point people edit, not a choice they have to make.

The three-step pattern then became GoCo’s standard for complex setup: onboarding, survey creation and benefits enrollment all adopted it. A fix for one feature became how the product handles complexity.

Reflection

Enterprise users want depth. They just want to choose when to reach it.

If I ran Reports again, I’d A/B test the defaults instead of shipping one set. A 20-person company and a 500-person company ask very different questions, and one set of defaults can’t serve both equally.
